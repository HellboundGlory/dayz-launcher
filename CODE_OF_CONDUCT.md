# Tetra Launcher Code of Conduct

Tetra Launcher is an open-source community project built around DayZ, Steam Workshop integration, and making the process of finding, verifying, and joining servers better.

Contributions, bug reports, feature ideas, testing, documentation, and constructive discussion are welcome regardless of experience level.

The goal of this Code of Conduct is simple: **keep the project useful, welcoming, technically productive, and enjoyable to work on.**

## Our Standards

When participating in the Tetra Launcher project, please:

* Treat contributors, users, maintainers, server owners, and other community members with respect.
* Keep criticism focused on code, ideas, behaviour, or technical decisions rather than the person behind them.
* Be patient with people who have different levels of programming, Linux, Windows, Steam, DayZ, or open-source experience.
* Give constructive feedback and explain the reasoning behind suggested changes where possible.
* Assume good faith unless there is a clear reason not to.
* Accept constructive feedback when reviewing or contributing code.
* Keep issues and pull requests relevant to the topic being discussed.
* Provide useful reproduction steps, logs, system information, or other details when reporting bugs where possible.
* Disclose security vulnerabilities privately as described in [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md).
* Respect the privacy of other users and contributors.
* Respect DayZ server communities even when discussing problems involving a particular server, mod, or configuration.
* Follow GitHub's Terms of Service and applicable laws.

Technical disagreement is completely acceptable. Personal hostility is not.

## Unacceptable Behaviour

The following behaviour is not acceptable in Tetra Launcher community spaces:

* Harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, or targeted abuse.
* Personal attacks, insults, deliberately inflammatory comments, or sustained hostility.
* Discrimination or derogatory comments based on characteristics such as race, nationality, ethnicity, disability, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, religion, or age.
* Sexual harassment or unwanted sexualized language or imagery.
* Publishing or threatening to publish someone's private information without permission.
* Repeatedly derailing issues, discussions, or pull requests.
* Spamming, advertising, or repeatedly promoting unrelated projects or services.
* Deliberately submitting misleading bug reports or fabricated technical information.
* Impersonating Tetra Launcher maintainers, contributors, or official project accounts.
* Encouraging harassment, review bombing, raids, or other targeted action against DayZ servers, server owners, mod authors, contributors, or other communities.
* Using the project community to organize cheating, credential theft, malware distribution, exploitation of third-party systems, or other malicious activity.
* Knowingly submitting malicious code, dependencies, binaries, links, or other content.
* Publicly disclosing an unresolved security vulnerability after being asked to use the responsible disclosure process.
* Repeatedly contacting someone after they have clearly asked you to stop.

Disagreement with a design decision, rejected feature request, closed issue, moderation action, or declined pull request is **not** itself a violation. Discussion is welcome as long as it remains constructive.

## Issues and Bug Reports

The issue tracker exists to improve Tetra Launcher.

When opening or participating in an issue:

* Search for an existing issue before creating a duplicate where practical.
* Use a descriptive title.
* Explain what happened and what you expected to happen.
* Include reproduction steps when possible.
* Include relevant platform information such as Windows/Linux, launcher version, and logs where appropriate.
* Remove passwords, tokens, personally identifiable information, or other sensitive data from logs before posting them.
* Keep discussion related to the issue.
* Do not repeatedly reopen or recreate an issue simply because a maintainer reached a different conclusion.

Feature requests and criticism are welcome. Demands, harassment, and entitlement are not.

## Pull Requests and Code Review

Code review should improve the project rather than become a contest over who is right.

Contributors and reviewers should:

* Explain significant implementation decisions when useful.
* Review the code rather than attacking the contributor.
* Be willing to revise an approach when technical evidence supports doing so.
* Keep large unrelated changes separate where practical.
* Avoid intentionally obfuscated, malicious, or misleading changes.
* Clearly identify major behavioural or architectural changes.
* Respect established project architecture and contribution requirements unless the purpose of the change is specifically to improve them.

Maintainers may request changes, close pull requests, or reject an implementation even when the underlying idea is valid.

A rejected contribution is not a judgement of the contributor.

## DayZ Servers, Mods, and Third Parties

Tetra Launcher interacts with an ecosystem that includes DayZ servers, Steam Workshop content, mod authors, community tools, Discord, and other third-party services.

Discussion of those services is allowed when relevant to the project.

However:

* Do not use Tetra Launcher issues or discussions to attack a particular server or community.
* Do not post private information about server owners, players, or mod authors.
* Do not make unsupported accusations of malicious behaviour.
* Do not organize harassment or retaliation against third parties.
* Where a problem appears to belong to DayZ, Steam, a Workshop mod, or another service rather than Tetra Launcher, maintainers may redirect or close the report.

Technical evidence is encouraged when reporting interoperability problems.

## Security Issues

Potential security vulnerabilities must be handled according to [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md).

Do **not** post exploit details, proof-of-concept code, credentials, sensitive logs, or instructions for exploiting an unresolved vulnerability in a public GitHub issue or discussion.

Good-faith security research and responsible vulnerability reports are welcome.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to official Tetra Launcher community spaces, including:

* GitHub issues.
* Pull requests and code reviews.
* GitHub Discussions.
* Commit discussions.
* Project documentation and wiki content.
* Official project websites or communication channels.
* Other spaces where someone is acting as an official representative of Tetra Launcher.

Behaviour outside official project spaces may also be considered when it directly targets project participants or creates a credible safety or harassment concern within the Tetra Launcher community.

## Moderation and Enforcement

Project maintainers are responsible for interpreting and enforcing this Code of Conduct.

Depending on the situation, maintainers may:

1. Ask a participant to change their behaviour.
2. Edit, hide, or remove inappropriate content.
3. Lock or limit a discussion.
4. Issue a formal warning.
5. Temporarily restrict participation.
6. Permanently ban a participant from project community spaces.

Maintainers may also reject or remove contributions containing intentionally malicious, abusive, deceptive, or unsafe content.

Moderation decisions should be proportionate to the severity and history of the behaviour.

Serious behaviour such as credible threats, targeted harassment, publishing private information, intentionally distributing malware, or deliberately compromising users may result in an immediate permanent ban without previous warnings.

## Enforcement Guidelines

### 1. Informal Correction

For minor or unintentional problems, a maintainer may ask the participant to edit a comment, change their tone, stay on topic, or otherwise correct the behaviour.

No further action is normally required once the issue has been resolved.

### 2. Warning

For a clear violation or repeated minor violations, the participant may receive a formal warning.

Continued behaviour after a warning may result in temporary or permanent restrictions.

### 3. Temporary Restriction

Serious violations or repeated behaviour after warnings may result in temporary restrictions from issues, pull requests, discussions, or other project spaces.

Attempts to bypass those restrictions may result in a permanent ban.

### 4. Permanent Ban

A permanent ban may be used for:

* Sustained harassment.
* Credible threats.
* Doxxing or publication of private information.
* Malicious code or malware submissions.
* Attempts to compromise project users or infrastructure.
* Repeated violations after previous moderation.
* Other behaviour that creates a serious risk to the project or its community.

## Maintainer Discretion

No Code of Conduct can cover every situation.

Maintainers may take reasonable action against behaviour that is clearly harmful to the project or its participants even when the exact behaviour is not explicitly listed above.

Moderation should be based on protecting the community and keeping project collaboration productive rather than punishing disagreement.
